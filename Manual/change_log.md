# 3 August 2026 -- Richard
- The ```sigmoid``` antecedent lake volume curve in the [lake config file](SubDocs/config/LakeConfig.md) is evaluated as ```log10 V(z) = A / (H + exp(-k (z - z0))) + log10(Vf)```, but ```A``` was never a config key - it was hard coded as ```log10(Vc)```. That is correct for the curves taken from the original ADV assessment workbooks, where ```H``` (the "height adjustment factor") was hand-tuned until the curve passed through a chosen midpoint volume, and where ```Vc``` is therefore ```10 ** A``` rather than the true ceiling. It is wrong for a curve fitted as a standard logistic between a floor and a ceiling, which sets ```H = 1``` and needs ```A = log10(Vc) - log10(Vf)```.
- **Bryan now computes ```A``` from the ```Vf``` and ```Vc``` already in the file, using ```H``` to tell the two conventions apart.** Where ```H``` is 1, or is not given at all, the curve is read as the standard logistic and ```A = log10(Vc) - log10(Vf)```, so the ceiling is exactly ```Vc```. Where ```H``` is anything else the curve is read the original way, with ```A = log10(Vc)```. ```H``` may now be left out of the config file, as it does nothing for a fitted curve.
- **Old models are unaffected.** Their ```H``` is above 1, so they are read exactly as before and results do not move. This split is safe because ```H = 1``` can never be a deliberate hand-tuned curve: in that convention it puts the upper asymptote at ```Vc * Vf```, which needs a floor of 1 ML to mean anything, and any realistic floor gives an ```H``` well above 1 (a 10,000 ML floor under a 140,000 ML ceiling gives 4.6).
- Getting the convention wrong fails silently, which is why it is worth knowing about. At Callide an ```H = 1``` file read the old way gave an ADV ceiling of 1.5 billion ML against a full supply volume of 129,041 ML. The run completed, but most realisations started above the top of the storage curve, so the lake level pinned at the top of the ```.els``` and the lake level frequency curve came out blank while the inflow curve still looked perfectly sensible.
- ```A``` can also be set explicitly in the ```coefficients```, overriding the rule above, for a curve that is neither convention. It is not needed for either of the standard cases and should normally be left out.
- Bryan now prints the resolved ```A```, which convention it came from, and both asymptotes of the curve when it loads a sigmoid layer. If the resulting ceiling lands more than a factor of two above ```Vc``` it also prints a warning saying what to check. Watch for this line when picking up an unfamiliar model.
- The ```Input MCDF``` key in the simulation list (```reservoir routing``` method) now accepts a ```.parquet``` file as well as a ```.csv```, matching the ```Inflow``` key, which already did. Both go through a shared reader. Previously an mcdf written as parquet by the Monte Carlo run had to be converted to csv by hand, and pointing at it directly failed with a ```UnicodeDecodeError```. Parquet needs ```pyarrow``` installed.

# 6 August 2026 -- Richard
- **Added the PMP embedded burst screen.** Every simulation that sets up extreme rainfall now checks each temporal pattern for embedded bursts that would exceed the PMP for their own sub-duration, and reports the result near the top of the simulation log. This is a different question from the sub-burst neutrality check: neutrality asks whether embedded bursts occur *more often* than the IFD statistics imply, and is answered from the simulated realisations; the PMP screen asks whether a pattern can produce a sub-burst above the physical envelope at all, and is answered from the patterns and the PMP depth-duration curve alone.
- The screen is a pre-simulation check because the breach condition does not depend on the sampling. For a pattern holding a fraction ```r_s``` of its depth in the worst window of sub-duration ```s```, the sub-burst reaches the sub-duration PMP once the burst depth exceeds ```D_crit = PMP_s / r_s```. Above the PMP anchor the sampled depth and the admissible bound (```PMP_s * max(1, depth / PMP_d)```) scale together, so the exceedance ratio stops growing and holds at ```PMP_d / D_crit```. That ratio is the ceiling on severity however far the sampling extrapolates past the AEP of the PMP, and a pattern with ```D_crit >= PMP_d``` never breaches at any depth.
- The report gives, for each pattern that can breach, the binding sub-duration, ```r_s```, ```D_crit```, the AEP at which the burst depth reaches it, the exceedance ratio at and above the anchor, and the share of that pattern set's sampled realisations above ```D_crit```. Patterns that can only breach beyond the sampled AEP range are reported as a ```CHECK```; patterns that breach inside it as a ```WARNING```.
- **Climate change is handled by uplifting the PMP depths along with the sampled depths**, each at its own duration's factor, since the rainfall uplift scales a duration's whole frequency curve by the same amount at every AEP. The factors used are printed above the table. A uniform uplift would leave both the exceedance ratio and the engagement AEP untouched; because the factor is duration dependent (15%/°C at 1 hour easing to 8%/°C at 24 hours and beyond) a sub-duration is uplifted more than its parent, so the ratio becomes ```r_s × PMP_d·u(d) / (PMP_s·u(s))``` and warming **eases** the bound. A climate change run is not the adverse case for this check.
- ARR areal/point as well as GSDM/GTSMR patterns are screened, each over the AEP window it is actually sampled in. For a small catchment the ARR patterns cannot get near the PMP and the screen is inert for them; the case worth watching is a catchment large enough that the [AEP of the PMP](SubDocs/config/IFDFilesConfig.md) is only half a decade beyond the 1 in 2,000 changeover, where the top of the ARR range does approach the PMP.
- **Nothing is filtered.** The screen only reports. If it fires on a GSDM/GTSMR pattern, note that those patterns also produce the deterministic PMF, so clipping them here but not there would lower the derived flood frequency curve while holding the PMF fixed and push the notional AEP of the PMF rarer for a procedural reason. See [the sub-burst check page](SubDocs/sub_burst_check.md) for how to read the result.
- The screen also validates the composite PMP depth-duration curve it depends on, warning if catchment-average PMP depth is not increasing with duration or PMP intensity is not decreasing. Both would make the ```PMP_s / PMP_d``` ratios meaningless.
- Tested against the Callide E010 storm data over all twelve production durations, sampling 1 in 1.58 to 1 in 4,194,304. 330 patterns screened; 40 breach within the sampled range at GWL 0 and 37 at GWL 2.7, all of them GSDM or GTSMR - **none of the 200 ARR patterns breaches**, the AEP of the PMP there being nearly three decades beyond the pattern changeover. Exceedances are small (median 2.1%, worst 10.1% at GWL 0) and nothing engages more frequently than 1 in 897,000 against an AEP of the PMP of 1 in 1.9 million. The binding sub-duration is consistently two thirds to three quarters of the parent duration, which is where the bound is inherently tight. The conclusion for Callide is **not to filter**: the exceedances are a structural consequence of applying an individual storm's temporal pattern to an envelope depth, they sit within the uncertainty of a generalised PMP estimate, and the 12 h GTSMR pattern 2 that produces Callide's own PMF is among the patterns that breach (by 1.8%).

# 21 August 2026 -- Richard
- **The ```reservoir routing``` method now analyses the inflow volumes.** Setting ```Analyse volumes``` to ```yes``` (or ```inflow```) in the simulation list adds a frequency analysis of the peak inflow volume of each analysis duration, on top of the peak inflow, level and outflow. It is analysed the same way the peaks are - by total probability theorem for Monte Carlo input, and by box plots, medians and critical durations for ensemble input - so the volume curves carry the same probabilities as the inflow curve beside them. Previously the method analysed peaks only, and the ```Analyse volumes``` key the other two methods read was ignored.
- Only the **inflow** volumes are analysed. The outflow and storage volumes of a routed run follow from the peak lake level and the rating curve, both of which are analysed already. The inflow volumes do not depend on the curve being routed - they are a property of the hydrographs the routing is handed - but a routed run is usually the only place stored hydrographs get analysed at all, and it is the volume rather than the peak that sizes the storage.
- **The analysis durations come from a ```volume_durations``` key in the method config file** - the one named by the row's ```Config file``` column, i.e. the [monte carlo config](SubDocs/config/MonteCarloConfig.md), not the main [simulation config file](SubDocs/config/SimsConfig.md) - in ```scheme_config``` or at the top level. Failing that it uses a ```storm_durations``` key, then the durations present in an ensemble input database, then the fixed 24, 36, 48, 72, 96, 120 hour list the monte carlo method uses. Where the durations came from is printed in the log. These are moving-window analysis durations, not storm durations. A duration that is not a whole number of timesteps, or that is longer than the hydrographs, is reported and skipped rather than silently producing nothing.
- Outputs go to the results folder: ```<Output file>__inflow_volumes<Output suffix>.csv``` holds every event's volume of every duration (with its assigned AEP for Monte Carlo input), alongside a quantile table and frequency curve per duration named like the peak ones, e.g. ```<Output file>__inflowVol72h_quantiles.csv```. Ensemble input gets the box plots, medians and critical durations under the usual ```plots``` and ```csv``` sub-folders, named ```<Output file><Output suffix>_inflowVol_*```.
- An analysis-only row (```Run models``` = ```no```) now needs its ```Inflow``` filepath when this is switched on, since the volumes are measured off the hydrographs rather than off the results database. It says so if the key is missing.
- **Fixed: the volume analysis window was a timestep short of the duration it was labelled with.** ```Simulator.analyse_volumes``` integrated over ```duration / timestep``` samples, which spans ```duration - timestep``` hours, so every volume the monte carlo and ensemble methods have reported was biased low by roughly ```timestep / duration``` - **1.0% on a 24 hour volume at a 15 minute timestep**, 0.3% at 72 hours, and worse at a coarser timestep. The window now spans the full duration (```duration / timestep + 1``` samples). **Volumes from earlier runs are therefore slightly lower than they should be; re-run the volume analysis of anything the numbers are being used from.** Peaks, levels and outflows are untouched - this only ever affected the volume analysis.
- The window now lives in one place, the new **```lib/Volumes.py```**, shared by all three methods, so it cannot drift again. It is vectorised over every simulation at once (a difference of cumulative sums rather than a ```rolling().apply()``` per column), which also makes the volume analysis of a large monte carlo run substantially quicker.
- Two silent failures in the volume analysis are now reported: a duration that is not a whole number of timesteps used to be dropped while the columns were still being named from the full list, which failed with a length mismatch that said nothing about durations; and a duration longer than the hydrographs used to write a column of blanks. Both are now skipped with a note saying which duration and why.
- ```analyse_ensemble``` in ```lib/EnbAnalysis.py``` now takes the columns to analyse, defaulting to the three peaks as before, which is how the ensemble volume analysis reuses it rather than repeating it.
- The run launcher keeps up with both: pre-flight now requires the ```Inflow``` file of a reservoir routing row that sets ```Analyse volumes```, even with ```Run models``` set to ```no``` (the volumes are measured off the hydrographs, so the usual analysis-only exemption does not apply), and the row's expected outputs include the volume table.
